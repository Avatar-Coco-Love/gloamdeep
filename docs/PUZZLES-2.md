# Puzzles, part 2

`PUZZLES.md` continued, under its rules (D-889): kept is not built, and
every name and number is a placeholder until its own step.

## C · Creatures as puzzle pieces
Every creature already has a way. Here the way is the tool, not only the
danger.

**C1 · The moth on the plate** Lampmoths and bats walk to the nearest lit
torch when the lantern is off (seeks light). Set a torch down on a
plate, switch off, and the moth flies over and holds the plate down. A
plate too far to reach yourself is opened by a creature you can steer.
*Uses* torches, seeks light, the lantern switch. *Needs* a plate tile.
*Fits* biome 1.

**C2 · The grub's aim** A spit grub fires at the lit lantern down a row
or column, and its glob hits whatever it meets. Stand so a crystal, a
creature or a hanging lamp is between you and the grub, and the glob
breaks it for you. Switch off to stop it firing. *Uses* spits, the
lantern switch. *Needs* something the glob can break. *Fits* depth 4
down.

**C3 · Which flask is real?** A shelf room full of flasks, most of them
mimics. Small tells give them away: a mimic has no shadow, or its glass
doesn't catch the lantern's light, or the guide won't dart to it. Bump
the wrong one and it wakes. A little detective puzzle; pillar 3 rewards
the player who looks closely. *Uses* mimics, the guide, the Bestiary's
"how it acts". *Needs* a tell drawn on mimics. *Fits* depths 3 to 5.

**C4 · Statues that move in the dark** Old warden statues stand still
while lit. Each turn the lantern is off, or each turn they are outside
its light, they take a step. Cross a hall of them, choosing when to go
dark to save oil and when to keep them frozen. They turn oil into the
puzzle's clock. *Uses* wardens (statues that sleep), light, oil. *Needs*
a new way: "moves only unlit". *Fits* the dungeon, Underhearth.

**C5 · Bones and timing** A beaten bone rat's pile stands up whole 3
turns later. A pile lying on a plate holds it down only until the rat
rises: 3 turns to get through the gate. Step on the pile and it's gone
for good, so you choose a safe path or a fast one. *Uses* bone rats,
plates. *Needs* a plate tile. *Fits* depth 4 down.

## G · Gates you come back to
Decided in outline: better tools and found abilities open places already
seen (D-450, D-463). These make that feel like a reward for remembering.

**G1 · Cracked walls, webs and roots** A family of barriers, each opened
by a different thing. Cracked walls need a pick. Thick webs burn with a
set torch, and the fire spreads to webs beside them, so a wall of web
can be burned from one end. Roots need an axe. Deep water needs a rod's
hook or, later, swimming. *Uses* the pick, torches, the rod, rubble.
*Needs* barrier tiles, fire spreading for webs. *Fits* every biome,
more in each.

**G2 · The marked map** The world map marks barriers you've seen but
couldn't pass, with a small sign for each kind. When you get the tool or
ability that opens them, the signs light up: "come back later" becomes a
list you can see. *Uses* the world map, the Journal. *Needs* map marks.
*Fits* the whole game.

**G3 · Biome 1's found ability, a puzzle verb** Biome 1's found ability
isn't decided yet (D-463). Whatever it is, it could be a new puzzle
verb, not only a way over gaps. Three examples, all placeholders: a
**leap** over a 1-tile gap; a **flare**, thrown light that lands 3 tiles
away (lights a far brazier, scares a shade); or **hold breath**, walking
through gloam for 3 turns. *Uses* D-463, one found ability per biome.
*Needs* its own decision, with the boss. *Fits* biome 1's bottom.

## K · Knowledge and lore
Puzzles where the answer is found elsewhere in the world (pillar 3).

**K1 · The mural holds the answer** A mural or note in one room shows the
order to light braziers, or which statue to bump, in another room. The
Lore tab keeps a copy once read, so you never have to write it down.
Lore stops being only story and becomes useful. *Uses* the Lore tab
(D-464), braziers (L1). *Needs* lore pages that carry a clue. *Fits*
the crypt, Underhearth.

**K2 · The waystone names** Each waystone carries a lamplighter's name
carved on it (`LORE.md`). A sealed vault deep down asks for names in
order, learned by waking waystones on the old road. It ties exploring to
the Lightkeepers' story. *Uses* waystones, the lore. *Needs* a name
picker on the vault door. *Fits* late game.

**K3 · Warmer, colder** The guide already brightens near the First Light
and dims near the gloam. In a vault room it glows brighter as you near
the hidden switch: the hint is in the light, not in words. *Uses* the
guide. *Needs* a hidden switch. *Fits* every biome.
