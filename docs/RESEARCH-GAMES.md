## The games
**Hoplite** (phone, turn-based, hex grid; from the 2013 7-Day Roguelike)
- Few enemies, each with one clear job: one fights close, one shoots, one
  throws bombs, one fires a beam. You can read what each will do, so every
  move is a small puzzle. Only 4 kinds, and reviews still praised it.
- Few actions, each with a cost: throwing the spear leaves you unarmed
  until you pick it up again; the shield bash pushes enemies and bombs.
- An altar on each floor: pick one reward, healing or a new power.
- Pushing enemies into hazards (lava) is part of fighting.

**Into the Breach**: enemies show what they will do before they do it, so
the game is reacting to clear threats. The most recommended "game like
Hoplite".

**Golden Krone Hotel**: light is a weapon. Sunlight hurts vampires, and
you can snuff out torches to hide. Close to pillar 1.

**Darkest Dungeon** (from general knowledge): a torch meter where lower
light means more danger and better loot.

**Shattered Pixel Dungeon** (the best-loved phone roguelike)
- A small backpack, and add-on bags (seeds, scrolls, wands) that save
  space.
- Crafting from finds: three seeds in an alchemy pot brew a potion.
- Hunger pushes you on, as oil does here.

**Desktop Dungeons**: uncovering dark tiles heals you, so exploring pays.

**Sproggiwood, Rogue Wizards**: a home town built up with tamed creatures
and helpful NPCs, and pets that join you. A reviewer found Sproggiwood's
town only decoration: a home base should do something.

## More games to try (2026-10-26, not yet played)
- **Brogue CE** (PC, free): light matters; glowing fungus lights caves,
  you hide in shadow, captives freed become allies. Closest on light.
- **Don't Starve**: the dark kills; you tend a light while you gather
  and craft.
- **Dungeon of the Endless**: rooms are powered to light them; dark rooms
  spawn monsters (the battery idea).
- **Tangledeep** (PC, Switch): a turn-based grid, a town, pets, taming,
  a small garden.
- **Caves of Qud**: creatures built from parts, limbs injured, named
  "legendary" creatures.
- **Slay the Spire**: every enemy shows its next move as an icon.
- **Hades**: death sends you home; friendships grow between runs.
- **Moonlighter**: dive, then sell the loot in your town shop.
- **Spelunky 2**: always deeper, with secret paths.
- **Dredge**: a boat, dangerous nights, a mystery uncovered slowly.
- **Hollow Knight**: fast-travel stations; abilities open new paths.
If only three: Brogue CE, Tangledeep, Don't Starve.

## How games do bosses (2026-10-26)
The [Boss Study](https://claude.ai/artifact/QiDD51bkLeTwgWwojJikJs)
compares 20 games side by side (≈ numbers are unchecked estimates).
1. A boss at each milestone (Shattered Pixel Dungeon every 5th floor).
2. It shows its next move (Slay the Spire, Into the Breach; D-349).
3. It tests the region's lesson (Shattered Pixel Dungeon, Hollow Knight).
4. The room fights too: push it into lava or water (Into the Breach).
5. Light decides the fight: Darkest Dungeon's Shambler comes only in
   total darkness. Rare elsewhere, so open ground for Gloamdeep.
6. Built from parts (Dwarf Fortress's forgotten beasts, Qud's legends).
7. Seen coming, so you prepare (Slay the Spire's map, Don't Starve's
   seasons).
8. No bosses; depth is the climax (Hoplite, Brogue, Stardew's mines).
9. Bosses who talk and remember (Hades).
10. Rematches and harder versions (Hades, Hollow Knight).
